## Background
This document outlines a process by which student leaders within a particular faculty can conduct an audit for how students are represented within programs, departments, and the entire faculty.

**Who?** While any student can conduct this audit, the individuals to whom this work is most relevant would be student leaders within the faculty (e.g., leaders of an academic club for a program in the faculty; student representatives within one of the departments)

**What?** The audit consists of determining the specific clubs and student representation roles within each department, program, and faculty.

**Why?** The benefits of this audit are to identify networking and collaboration options and to compare the different practices for student representation, which may help in identifying potential improvements.

**How?** See the steps below for conducting the audit.

## Content
Here are the key steps to conducting the audit:
1.	Select a student leader who is committed to conducting the audit.
2.	Plan how you will be tracking the information for the audit (see next page for an example table)
3.	Reach out to the faculty to explain the audit
    - a.	Explain why and how you are conducting an audit
    - b.	Consider feedback about the process 
    - c.	Ask about how students may be represented at the faculty level (e.g., faculty council, faculty club)
    - d.	Ask for support in connecting with individual departments and programs to complete the audit
4.	Connect with individual program and departments to collect the following information:
    - a. What is the structure of how students are represented within the program/department?
    - b. Who are the current representatives?
5.	Connect with the student representatives and clubs to gather additional contextual information.
    - a. What are the strengths and weaknesses of the current structure?
    - b. What are suggestions for improvement?
