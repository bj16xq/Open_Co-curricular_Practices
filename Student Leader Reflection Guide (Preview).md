# Student Leader Reflection Guide

> “The unexamined life is not worth living” 
> — Socrates, as cited by Plato, n.d., Apology (38a)

## Overview 
There are no widely-discussed standards for student leaders and student representatives when concluding a term (whether the incumbent is returning to the position or is transitioning an incoming person). Consequentially, there is under-realized potential to engage in reflection activities that consolidate insights from one’s experience and inform improvements for the future. To address this gap, this guide provides an explanation for student leaders and representatives in how to reflect on their practice (i.e., student leader/representative duties) so as to catalyse insights for dialogue on future improvement.

## Reflection Prompts

A useful reflection strategy is start/stop/continue. [Video link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=igg2LC6-iXY&pp=ygUTc3RhcnQvc3RvcC9jb250aW51ZQ%3D%3D). The process is to ask yourself:

What practices should I/we **start** doing that I/we haven't done yet?
What practices should I/we **stop** doing that aren’t working?
What practices should I/we **continue** doing that are working?

**I** = yourself as a student leader  **We** = the student group as a whole
**Practices** = both formal processes as well as informal habits.

In addition from answering the **what**, you should also ask yourself **why** you should start/stop/continue that particular aspect of your practice to determine underlying interactions. Also, consider any potential latent effects/consequences, whether favourable or not.

For specificity, this reflection can be broken down into each of the various aspects of your role. For example, you should reflect separately for meetings, social/engagement events, services/initiatives, and communications:
- What should I/we start/stop/continue for meetings? Why? Will there be any notable consequences for doing so?
- What should I/we start/stop/continue for social/engagement events? Why? Will there be any notable consequences for doing so?
- What should I/we start/stop/continue for services/initiatives? Why? Will there be any notable consequences for doing so?
- What should I/we start/stop/continue for communications? Why? Will there be any notable consequences for doing so?

### Student Representatives (Department/Program/Faculty)
📄 What departmental/program/faculty policies are you still unfamiliar with?

📢 How strong are communications to students?

❓ What questions do you have about the department/program committee structure?

📅 What kinds of events might engage students? What barriers to participation may exist for them and how can we overcome them?

💲 If you had a $500 budget, what would you spend it on?

📚 Do we have resources in our department space? (e.g., printer, computers, pencils, pens, paper, etc. Do students know about them? How useful have they been? What could be added or modified?

### Club Leaders (Department/Program/Faculty)
📄 What departmental/program/faculty policies are we still unfamiliar with? Have we taken note of any policy of which there is less awareness among students in your program/faculty or that affects your club somehow?

📢 How strong is our communication with students/members? What should we stop, start, and continue?

👥 How might we collaborate with other clubs?

👥 How might we collaborate with the department/program/faculty student representatives?

📅 What kinds of events might engage students? What barriers to participation may exist for them and how can we overcome them?

## Club or Student Union Leadership Role Ideas

In the description of club or union leadership (e.g., board or senator) positions (e.g., in the club or union constitution/charter), consider adding a list of optional/flexible duties such that not all have to be completed, but a certain number of them must be done by the end of the term. For example, incumbents can have the choice of completing 3 out of the following 7 support tasks:
1.	Attending 4 social events
2.	Organising a social event
3.	Facilitating collaboration or partnership for an event or service
4.	Recruiting at least 3 new members, event attendees, or program participants
5.	Creating content for social media
6.	Providing an insightful recommendation for a club constitution/charter amendment
7.	Working a shift for a tabling initiative

## Club or Student Union Election Ideas
This section discusses ideas to support the election/appointment process for new student leaders and transitions thereof, within the context of a club or student union. The overall idea comprises the outgoing student leaders (who aren’t seeking re-election) creating scenario-based questions that prompt candidates to synthesize their knowledge and skillset to support the membership in making an informed vote. This process also challenges candidates to become maximally informed about the role and their alignment thereto.

**Process:**
1.	In advance of nominations for elections, outgoing executives create hypothetical scenarios that convey scenarios similar to those they encountered in the position, accompanied by questions to prompt critical thinking about that scenario.
2.	Following election nominations, namely during the candidate panel/Q&A, these scenarios and questions are provided to showcase how well candidates can thoughtfully engage with the scenarios.
3.	Once the next executives are chosen, a transition meeting can be held where similar scenarios are discussed to help the incoming executive(s) prepare for their role.
