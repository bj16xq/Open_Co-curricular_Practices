## Background
Student unions have the purpose of serving and advocating for the student body at an institution. As student unions are often non-profit corporations, there is a board of directors providing oversight of finances, internal policy, and more. These responsibilities should not be taken lightly, and measures for accountability are crucial. This resource proposes a policy amendment by which a student union board of directors can amend their internal policy to mandate video recording of meetings to enhance transparency.

## Proposed Policy Language
> "Each meeting of the Board of Directors shall be captured in a video recording that will be made accessible to the membership and stakeholders, with the exception of in-camera items."

## Rationale
The board is supposed to be transparent and accountable to students. This amendment will allow for students to not only become more familiar with the Board and its business, but they will be more informed when considering candidacy. Regarding financial transparency, students should not just know where their money is spent, but how and when those decisions were made. One possible disadvantage is that board members may be concerned about privacy. However, given that a board of directors holds the highest level of responsibility within an organization, transparency is paramount. For student board members (or prospective ones) whose concerns for privacy are so severe that recording meetings would preclude their participation, perhaps the board role is not meant for them, and they can consider other ways of contributing to the student union in a manner that is private. Facing good faith public scrutiny as a member of an important decision making body is not the same as facing harassment. While public scrutiny can become harassment and defamation in some instances, it is very implausible for the above policy amendment to substantially heighten risk of harassment or defamation above and beyond the existing risk in a board position prior to implementing the above amendment. Consider how many of politicians’ duties are broadcasted (e.g., sessions of parliament) and thus subject to public criticism. 

Two examples of recorded board meetings are the Laurier Students' Union and Brock University Students’ Union. The Laurier Students' Union has recorded their Board meetings since before the pandemic. Currently, one must login with a Laurier student account to view their Board meetings. However, the recordings used to be on their Facebook page until early 2020, and received hundreds of views ([click here for Facebook page](https://www.facebook.com/pg/LaurierStudentsUnion/videos/?ref=page_internal)). Since then, recordings have transferred to a student portal. The Brock University Students’ Union currently posts their board meeting videos on YouTube.

## Execution
Meetings can be recorded using the record function of the virtual platform (e.g., Microsoft Teams). In-person meetings can be recorded on a camera or laptop webcam. There are multiple possible locations to host video recordings. The recordings can be stored on a SharePoint site so that only those with university email accounts can view the recording. Alternatively, they can be put on YouTube.
